The latest in Northeast Bellevue

November 12, 2026

Cherry Crest Park, at 12404 NE 32nd Street, is 5.09 acres in the northwest corner of the Cherry Crest neighborhood, with three tennis courts, trails and natural green space. Source

November 5, 2026

The Kelsey Creek Park trail starts from 410 130th Place SE, at the bridge over Kelsey Creek. Its gravel loop follows a bowl-shaped wetland in the flood plain, suits walking, jogging and biking, and is open year-round. Source

October 29, 2026

Ivanhoe Park, at 16600 Northup Way, is 2.48 acres with play areas for older and younger children, a basketball court, picnic tables and walking paths. Source

October 22, 2026

The missing sidewalk on the east side of 173rd Avenue NE, Northup Way to NE 15th Place, is still in design, with construction expected to start in spring 2029. Residents favored raised crosswalks and speed cushions for the traffic calming that goes with it. Source

October 15, 2026

Tam O'Shanter Park, at 1655 173rd Avenue NE, has 1.5 miles of trail through 15 acres of Douglas fir and bigleaf maple, and its trails link north to Redmond's Viewpoint Park. Source

October 8, 2026

Corridor safety improvements on 164th Avenue NE from Northup Way to NE 30th Street are funded in every scenario ($4.3 million). So are the crosswalk upgrades at NE 28th Street and Bel-Red Road, and at NE 24th Street and 171st Avenue NE. The base budget funds part of the crosswalk construction ($1.8 million); the sales-tax scenarios complete it ($2 million). Source

October 1, 2026

A cautionary permit story on 28th Place. The city approved a critical-areas permit with conditions at 17041 NE 28th Place to resolve two retaining walls and a gravel area built without permits — part on private property, part in unimproved right-of-way, all inside a steep-slope buffer and a stream setback. The right-of-way portion is to be restored to its prior condition, and the case sits alongside an open enforcement action. File 25-124402-LO. Source

September 24, 2026

About 1,100 feet of missing sidewalk is being filled in on the east side of 173rd Avenue NE, from Northup Way to NE 15th Place, paid from the area's $366,000 enhancement allocation plus Neighborhood Sidewalk Program money. Source

September 17, 2026

Your local streets were in the first wave of 20 mph signs. At Ardmore Park, the resident-voted Neighborhood Enhancement project is clearing ivy and blackberry and planting native trees at the south end, with completion set for December 2028. Source

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Bellevue newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Bellevue that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Bellevue's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
