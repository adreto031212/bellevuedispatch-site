The latest in Bridle Trails

November 12, 2026

Bridle Trails Corner Park, at NE 24th Street and 134th Avenue NE, has a forest loop path, a wildflower meadow, native gardens with wetland views, a large lawn, picnic tables and a small playground for ages 2 to 5. Source

November 5, 2026

Bridle Trails Valley Creek Park, at 4432 140th Ave NE, has a loop trail, a playground, a covered picnic shelter for drop-in use, a restroom and a 12-stall parking lot. Source

October 29, 2026

The city plans to turn the operations center at the Bellevue Golf Course, 5500 140th Avenue NE, into one hub for park maintenance crews. The cost estimate came in over budget at 60% design, and redesign found more than $3.4 million in savings. On Tuesday's consent agenda: $301,710 more for the architect to redraw the bid set. Source

October 22, 2026

Bridle Trails Valley Creek Park, at 4432 140th Avenue NE, is 1.65 acres with an adventure play area, a picnic shelter and a loop trail around a lawn. It sits beside five acres of protected wetland, near the Bellevue Golf Course. Source

October 15, 2026

Bridle Trails now has a date for its neighborhood plan rewrite: council has scheduled it for 2028–2029, after Lake Hills and West Lake Sammamish (now under way) and Woodridge and West Bellevue (2027–2028). Source

October 8, 2026

Two projects on the city's ten-year transportation list are funded in all three budget scenarios. A sidewalk on NE 40th Street from 140th to 148th Avenue NE would be built in full, at $6.2 million. Crosswalks and speed reduction on 132nd Avenue NE near Bridle Trails would get design money only, $150,000. Source

October 1, 2026

The four gateway signs that mark the entries to Bridle Trails are being replaced this year, and installation is scheduled for November. Residents voted the project onto the Neighborhood Enhancement Program list and then picked the design; the new signs use stone cladding, brick or concrete with metal elements, since wood is not allowed under the city's maintenance rules. Source

September 24, 2026

Goff Creek gets a cleanup this fall: ivy pulled, slope stabilized and natives planted across roughly 8,000 square feet at NE 24th Street between 130th Place NE and 131st Place NE, to slow runoff and erosion. Up to four replacement neighborhood identity signs are due by December, with resident input on the design. Source

September 17, 2026

Your local streets were in the first wave of 20 mph signs. Separately, Saturday September 26 is National Public Lands Day, one of twelve 2026 dates when Bridle Trails State Park and every other state park waive the Discover Pass. Source

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Bellevue newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Bellevue that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Bellevue's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
