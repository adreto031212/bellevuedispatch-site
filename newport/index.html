The latest in Newport

November 12, 2026

Newport Hills Park, at 6029 120th Avenue SE, covers 7.82 acres with a lighted soccer field, a ballfield, a play area, restrooms and a 33-space lot. Source

November 5, 2026

Coal Creek Natural Area trailhead parking is on Coal Creek Parkway between Forest Drive and SE 60th Street. The moderate 1.5-to-3-mile hike connects to the Red Town Trailhead on Cougar Mountain. Source

October 29, 2026

Newport Hills Woodlawn Park, at 11560 SE 60th Street, has 10.7 acres of forested loop trail, a 1.25-acre fenced off-leash dog area, a playground, a picnic shelter and on-site parking. Source

October 22, 2026

Newcastle Beach Park, at 4400 Lake Washington Boulevard SE, is Bellevue's largest beach park, with a 300-foot dock, a nature trail and a launch for kayaks and other non-motorized boats. Open dawn to dusk. Source

October 15, 2026

Crews are pulling blackberry, ivy and invasive weeds out of the forested parts of the Newport Hills Property, 11450 SE 60th Street, and replanting native trees and shrubs. Residents voted the project onto the neighborhood's $282,000 budget; it is expected to finish in December 2027. Source

October 8, 2026

Two speed-reduction projects are funded in all three budget scenarios, at $200,000 each: 116th Avenue SE south of SE 60th Street, and 124th Avenue SE north of Coal Creek Parkway. Source

October 1, 2026

The city has issued an amended decision approving, with conditions, the expanded safe parking facility at Newport Presbyterian Church, 4010 120th Avenue SE, along with a modified SEPA determination. The original July 29 approval and its environmental determination were withdrawn and reissued after the Hearing Examiner dismissed administrative appeals on September 16 for lack of jurisdiction. Files 26-102130-LR and 26-102178-LM; there is no administrative appeal, and the deadline to file in King County Superior Court is October 14. Source

September 24, 2026

The crosswalk at SE 60th Street and 120th Avenue SE, at the northeast corner of Newport Hills Park, is getting pedestrian-activated flashing beacons on both sides, upgraded street lighting and a new curb ramp on the north side, with completion set for late 2026. Newport residents picked it from ten ballot options; 498 ballots came back. Source

September 17, 2026

Council adopted the HOMA code amendment on March 17, allowing five- to six-story mixed-use buildings at neighborhood centers including the Newport Hills Shopping Center site. The community club reports no redevelopment application has been filed. Source

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Bellevue newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Bellevue that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Bellevue's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
