The latest in BelRed

November 12, 2026

Bel-Red Mini Park, at 12300 Bel-Red Road, is a 0.31-acre stop with a picnic area, open dawn to dusk. Source

November 5, 2026

The city is planning a new park at 12525 NE Spring Blvd, funded by the 2022 Park and Open Space Levy. The planning process runs about a year, and a third community meeting is set for autumn 2026. Source

October 29, 2026

The city approved its own plan to turn a 2-acre gravel parking area at 12525 NE Spring Boulevard and 12755 NE 14th Street into a temporary yard for maintenance materials and vehicles (file 26-110469-GD). Appeals close at 5 p.m. November 4. Source

October 22, 2026

The Planning Commission holds its public hearing on the BelRed Look Forward code rewrite Wednesday, October 28, 6:30 p.m. at City Hall. It rewrites how BelRed development is regulated, adds an affordable housing program, and lets development rights from rural King County land be used in BelRed. Written comments go to PlanningCommission@bellevuewa.gov by 5 p.m. that day. Source

October 15, 2026

The deal for Sound Transit's parking garage at the 130th light rail station, and the housing and shops planned around it, is expected to reach council in the first quarter of 2027, according to the city's economic development update. Source

October 8, 2026

Wright Runstad's request to turn the Spring District's north blocks from office into housing went to a virtual public meeting Wednesday night. Comments are accepted until the city issues a decision, so there is still time to weigh in: file 26-120531-LP, planner Jim Harris, jaharris@bellevuewa.gov. Separately, the scenarios council saw Tuesday differ on Spring Boulevard between 124th and 130th Avenues NE. The base budget pays for part of construction ($38 million), and the sales-tax scenarios complete it ($74.8 million). Meeting notice · Scenarios

October 1, 2026

Koz on BelRed is approved. The city issued design review approval with conditions for a six-story, 188-unit building at 13800 NE Bellevue-Redmond Road — 139 micro-apartments and 49 one- and two-bedroom units, 35 parking stalls, and 143 of the 188 units designated affordable. File 23-111025-LD; the appeal period closed September 30. Source

September 24, 2026

The BelRed Arts development agreement passed council unanimously: BRIDGE Housing leases a city-owned site long term, and about 60 of the project's 172 units are affordable, with creative space and an artist focus. Source

September 17, 2026

The townhome project at 12727–12729 Northup Way has been cut from 91 units to 36 across six buildings and re-noticed by the city as SpringVue Townhomes. It sits about three blocks from the Spring District station. Source

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Bellevue newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Bellevue that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Bellevue's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
