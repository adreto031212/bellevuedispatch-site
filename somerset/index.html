The latest in Somerset

November 5, 2026

Somerset Greenbelts, at 4738 136th Avenue SE, has 6 acres of maintained trails with signed routes. Its trails connect to Westwood Highlands Greenbelt and Forest Hill Park. Source

October 29, 2026

Forest Hill Neighborhood Park, at 13232 SE 51st Place, is 5.36 acres of play area, picnic tables and walking trails, tied into the Somerset Greenbelts trail system. There is no parking; walk in. Source

October 22, 2026

Construction has started on the Forest Drive trailhead near Coal Creek Parkway: accessible parking, paved paths to the existing trails, signs, benches and landscaping. The trails stay open, and the work runs through the end of the year. Source

October 15, 2026

Coal Creek Natural Area, one of the greenbelts on the Somerset side of the city, has 4.5 miles of trail through second-growth forest, with remnants of the area's early coal-mining industry along the way. The main trailhead is the Cinder Mine Trailhead on Newcastle Golf Club Road. Source

October 8, 2026

Pedestrian improvements on Somerset Drive are on the ten-year list, but only in the scenario that adds the $20 vehicle fee ($2 million). They are not in the base budget or the sales-tax-only scenario. Source

October 1, 2026

Somerset Boulevard SE between SE Newport Way and SE 43rd Street is in design for a safety package: sidewalk, crosswalks, traffic calming and related improvements, with the city still evaluating alternatives before it settles on a design. It is funded through the transportation levy's neighborhood safety program. Source

September 24, 2026

A $217,000 project is pulling blackberry and ivy out of city-managed greenbelts across Somerset Park, Forest Park Open Space, Forest Park Meadows, Eastgate Park, Westwood Highlands and the Coal Creek Natural Area, replanting natives that crews maintain until established. Completion is set for fall 2027; 463 Somerset ballots picked it. Source

September 17, 2026

The school district's 10-acre Somerset II parcel near Forest Drive is now under contract to Tri Pointe Homes at $19 million, down from $30 million after environmental review reduced the buildable lot count. Closing follows preliminary plat approval, expected late 2027 to spring 2028. Source

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Bellevue newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Bellevue that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Bellevue's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
