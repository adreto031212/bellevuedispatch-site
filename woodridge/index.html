The latest in Woodridge

November 12, 2026

Norwood Village Neighborhood Park, at 12309 SE 23rd Place, is 1.65 acres with two tennis courts lined for pickleball, a playground, a picnic area and a basketball and multi-purpose court. Source

November 5, 2026

Woodridge Water Tower Park, at 1843 125th Avenue SE, is 1.33 acres with a trail shaded by tall trees, and it is known for its blue water tower. Source

October 29, 2026

Woodridge's plan rewrite is next in line: council has directed the city to finish Woodridge and West Bellevue in 2027–2028, and Wednesday's commission memo says planning for those two areas starts in fall 2027. Source

October 22, 2026

Woodridge's next neighborhood enhancement round runs April to November 2028, with $205,000 for projects residents vote on. The last round, $161,000 from 346 ballots, paid for trail drainage, entrance landscaping on 121st Avenue SE and new streetlights. Source

October 15, 2026

Woodridge's neighborhood plan rewrite is scheduled for 2027–2028, alongside West Bellevue. The plans cover neighborhood identity, gathering places, getting around and the natural environment. Source

October 8, 2026

Multimodal improvements on SE 8th Street, from 112th Avenue SE to the Lake Hills Connector, are partly funded in the base budget ($18.5 million) and completed in the sales-tax scenarios ($24.2 million). Intersection work where the Lake Hills Connector meets SE 8th Street is funded in all three scenarios ($6 million). Source

October 1, 2026

Woodridge Open Space at 12855 SE 17th Street is 20 acres of upland forest with a half-mile loop trail through fir, cedar and maple, over bridges and small streams and across steep slopes. It is part of the Richards Valley Greenway, and the trail links Woodridge to Richards Road, the Lake to Lake Trail and the Richards Valley trail system. Source

September 24, 2026

Richards Creek Open Space at 1640 Richards Road is a city-managed wetland site where the Neighborhood Enhancement Program cleared non-native plants from the wet areas and replanted natives for habitat. It is one of four projects Woodridge residents voted through on a $161,000 area budget, alongside trail drainage and surfacing on the Woodridge/McTavish trail. Source

September 17, 2026

Woodridge Water Tower Park is 1.33 acres at 1843 125th Avenue SE, named for the blue water tower that marks the top of the ridge, with a short shaded loop under tall trees. Woodridge's 20 mph signs arrive later in 2026. Source

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Bellevue newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Bellevue that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Bellevue's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
