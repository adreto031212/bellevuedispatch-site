The latest in West Lake Sammamish

November 12, 2026

Lake Sammamish Landing is a city rest stop on the water for kayaks, canoes and paddleboards between other launch sites. It has no launch, parking, restroom or dock, so the city asks paddlers not to start or end a trip there. Source

November 5, 2026

Sunrise Park, at 4095 W Lake Sammamish Parkway SE, is a city neighborhood park with a picnic area and trails. Source

October 29, 2026

No property owner in West Lake Sammamish or Lake Hills asked for a land use map change by the September 15 deadline for the plan rewrite. City planners walked public spaces in both areas October 3 and are walking the business districts with shop owners in late October. Source

October 22, 2026

Weowna Park, at 1420 168th Avenue SE, is 93 acres of trail through remnant old-growth forest, along Phantom Creek's waterfalls and pools, with views of Lake Sammamish and the Cascades. Source

October 15, 2026

The neighborhood plan rewrite is on a published schedule. Listening runs through December, a draft vision follows early next year, the Planning Commission hearing comes in September 2027, and council adoption is planned for December 2027. No property owner asked for a land use change by the September 15 deadline. Source

October 8, 2026

Phase 3 of West Lake Sammamish Parkway is completed in every scenario ($29 million). Phase 4 gets part of its construction money: $15 million in the base budget, $17.7 million with the sales tax. A sidewalk on SE 34th Street from 162nd Place SE to the parkway gets design money only in the base budget ($1.35 million) and is built in the sales-tax scenarios ($7.73 million). Source

October 1, 2026

Two items. The city approved a clearing and grading permit at 16814 SE 35th Street to cut two black poplars in a stream buffer inside a recorded Native Growth Protection Area; both are to be left standing as wildlife snags, with six native replacement trees planted, backed by an arborist report and risk assessments (file 26-114853-GJ). And West Lake Sammamish has begun its own neighborhood area plan, with a public space assessment walk on Saturday, October 3. Permit · Plan

September 24, 2026

The median at the crosswalk next to Spiritridge Park is being replanted this fall with low-maintenance natives and expanded irrigation. Two stretches of West Lake Sammamish Parkway SE — north of the French Immersion School and south of SE 40th Place — get the same treatment by spring 2027. Source

September 17, 2026

King County is designing the replacement of 4.5 miles of 50-year-old sewer pipe along West Lake Sammamish Parkway and into Redmond. As of the July update the work is still in design, with construction starting in 2029. Source

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Bellevue newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Bellevue that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Bellevue's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
